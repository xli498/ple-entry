   SECTION 1 — DESIGN BASIS
   1.1 General
   These calculations establish the design of the temporary support of excavation for the deep plant-room excavation
   planned beneath Block B of Package A of the Birchwood Village Expansion Program. The excavation is a
   rectangular pit approximately 68 ft × 44 ft in plan, cut into the podium footprint east of the shared Block A/B
   basement. Excavation commences from a working grade at El. 750.0 and terminates at a plant-room subgrade at
   El. 726.0, a total excavated depth of 24 ft.
   The temporary works described herein are the Contractor's means and methods and are supplied by delegated
   design under Article 3.1.B of Specification Section 31 50 00.

   1.2 Selected System
   A driven steel sheet-pile wall, restrained by a single row of pre-stressed tieback anchors installed near the top of
   the excavation, has been selected as the support system. The rationale is:
      • (a) The excavation is deeper than would be economically supported by a cantilever wall;
      • (b) A single-level tied wall provides adequate stiffness for the 24 ft depth without a soldier-pile / lagging
      system, and drives to competent stratigraphy without an oversized toe;
      • (c) A continuous sheet-pile section provides a substantially closed perimeter and does not rely on open-face
      lagging.
   Timber lagging with soldier piles was evaluated and rejected for this reason.

   3.3 Design Approach — Blended Profile
   For the calculation of the active pressure diagram behind the wall from working grade El. 750.0 down to
   subgrade El. 726.0, a blended profile is adopted using a weighted average total unit weight of 119 pcf and an
   equivalent effective friction angle of 30° (giving Ka = 0.333, Kp = 3.00). The blended profile provides a
   conservative envelope that:
       • (a) does not credit any cohesion contribution from the clay,
       • (b) treats both strata as frictional with a single unified Ka, and
       • (c) reproduces the vertical stress at subgrade within 1% of the layered calculation.
   This is a standard simplification for the design of temporary sheet-pile walls of this proportion. The
   blended-profile assumption is checked against a layered calculation in Section 5.3.

   these calculations assumes dry conditions from working grade El. 750.0 down to subgrade El. 726.0 for the

   The 250 psf construction surcharge governs and is carried as a uniform load applied at the ground surface

   perimeter zone within 10 ft of the wall by operational restriction; this restriction is to be reflected in the site

   4.2 Lateral Pressure from Surcharge
   Under Rankine theory with a uniform surface surcharge, the additional horizontal pressure applied to the wall is:
         p_q = Ka · q = 0.333 · 250 = 83 psf
   This 83 psf is constant with depth over the retained height (rectangular distribution, top to subgrade).

   excavation footprint is to be drawn down per the Contractor's dewatering plan (Reference R-3, Submittal

   SECTION 5 — LATERAL EARTH PRESSURE — ACTIVE SIDE
   5.1 Method
   Rankine active earth pressure theory is applied, with the blended profile per Section 3.3. The wall is assumed
   vertical, the ground surface horizontal, no wall friction, and no water-bearing pressure (per Section 4.3).

   5.2 Active Earth Pressure Distribution
   Adopted parameters:
       • γ_blended = 119 pcf
       • φ' = 30°, Ka = 0.333
       • Retained height H = 24 ft (El. 750.0 to El. 726.0)
       • Surcharge q = 250 psf
   At any depth z below the ground surface (measured downward from El. 750.0):
         p_a(z) = Ka · γ · z + Ka · q
                    = 0.333 · 119 · z + 0.333 · 250
                    = 39.6 · z + 83          [psf, z in ft]
   At the subgrade (z = 24 ft, El. 726.0):
        • Component from soil weight: 0.333 · 119 · 24 = 951 psf ≈ 950 psf
        • Component from surcharge: 0.333 · 250 = 83 psf
        • Total active pressure at subgrade: 950 + 83 = 1,033 psf
   The 950 psf effective lateral pressure at subgrade is the earth-pressure component and is the value carried forward
   as the governing lateral pressure at the excavated bottom under the drawn-down condition established in Section
   2 and Section 4.3.

   SECTION 6 — LATERAL EARTH PRESSURE — PASSIVE SIDE
   6.1 Passive Resistance Below Subgrade
   Below the subgrade at El. 726.0 and down to the sheet-pile toe at El. 722.0, passive earth pressure is mobilized on
   the inside face of the wall. The stratum is CL clay throughout this 4-ft interval. The design passive pressure is
   computed using the long-term drained parameters (φ' = 26°, Kp = 2.56, c' = 100 psf), which is conservative
   relative to the short-term undrained case for the anticipated 4- to 5-month duration of the temporary works.
   At depth d below subgrade (d in ft):
         p_p(d) = Kp · γ · d + 2 · c' · √Kp
                   = 2.56 · 120 · d + 2 · 100 · √2.56
                   = 307 · d + 320          [psf, d in ft]
   At the toe (d = 4 ft, El. 722.0):
         p_p(toe) = 307 · 4 + 320 = 1,228 + 320 = 1,548 psf
   Design passive pressure diagram (in front of wall, El. 726.0 down to El. 722.0):
         El. 726.0           _______
                             |        320 psf (cohesion rectangle)
                             |___________
                                       \
                                         \ frictional triangle
                                          \ (320 -> 1,548 psf)
                                           \
         El. 722.0           ______________\
                                            1,548 psf

   6.2 Design Passive Force per Foot of Wall
   Passive force per lineal foot of wall over the 4 ft embedment:
       • Rectangular (cohesion) contribution: 320 · 4 = 1,280 lb/ft
       • Triangular (frictional) contribution: 0.5 · (1,548 − 320) · 4 = 2,456 lb/ft
       • Total mobilizable passive resistance: P_p = 3,736 lb/ft ≈ 3,740 lb/ft

   6.3 Reduction Factor
   A factor of safety of 1.5 is applied to the mobilizable passive resistance for the design case (temporary works,
   the delegated designer’s adopted criteria):
         P_p,design = P_p / 1.5 = 3,740 / 1.5 = 2,493 lb/ft ≈ 2,490 lb/ft

   SECTION 8 — SLIDING AND OVERTURNING CHECK
   8.1 Sliding
   The wall does not translate horizontally as a rigid body because it is restrained by the tieback anchor at El. 745.5
   (see Section 10) and by the passive block below subgrade. The tieback horizontal reaction (15.4 kips/ft, per
   Section 7.3) is the direct restraint against horizontal translation of the upper wall; the passive block restrains
   translation of the toe. Sliding is therefore not a rigid-body failure mode for this wall configuration; the applicable
   checks are the anchor pull-out (Section 10) and the toe embedment (Section 9).
   For completeness, and treating the sheet-pile section conservatively as a floating rigid strip with only the passive
   block available for restraint (i.e., without any tieback contribution), the sliding factor of safety is:
         FS_sliding = P_p / P_A = 3,740 / (17,844 − 4,120 − 332)
                        = 3,740 / 13,392 = 0.28
   This is the "wall-free" sliding number and confirms that the tieback is essential to global stability. With the
   tieback engaged (Section 10), sliding is fully restrained.
   8.2 Overturning About the Toe
   The wall as a rigid strip pivots about the toe at El. 722.0. Taking moments about the toe:
   Total driving moment M_o about toe:

   SECTION 9 — TOE EMBEDMENT CHECK
   9.1 Free-Earth Support Method
   The wall is analyzed by the free-earth support method (USACE EM 1110-2-2504, Section 4-6) with the anchor as
   the upper reaction and the passive block below subgrade as the lower reaction.

   9.2 Required Embedment
   Taking moments about the tieback anchor and equating driving and resisting moments (with the required factor of
   safety applied to the passive resistance):
         Required moment from passive = 1.5 · M_driv_below_subgrade
             + full driving moment above subgrade / anchor arm ratio
   Working the free-earth equation for required embedment d below subgrade:
   The controlling closed-form for d (per USS Sheet Piling Design Manual §7.2, single-anchor case, cohesive soil
   below dredge line with Kp = 2.56, c' = 100 psf, γ = 120 pcf) reduces to:
         (1/6) · Kp · γ · d³ + c' · √Kp · d² − M_A / z_A = 0
   Solving iteratively with M_A = 188,223 ft-lb/ft and z_A = 23.5 ft (arm from toe to tieback):
         Trial d = 4.0 ft:         (1/6)(2.56)(120)(64) + (100)(1.6)(16) − (188,223/23.5)
                                   = 3,277 + 2,560 − 8,010 = −2,173               (deficit)
         Trial d = 4.5 ft:         (1/6)(2.56)(120)(91.1) + (100)(1.6)(20.25) − 8,010
                                   = 4,664 + 3,240 − 8,010 = −106                 (essentially balanced)
         Trial d = 4.6 ft:         (1/6)(2.56)(120)(97.3) + (100)(1.6)(21.16) − 8,010
                                   = 4,982 + 3,386 − 8,010 = +358                 (satisfied)
   Required d_min ≈ 4.5 ft. Provided d = 4.0 ft (from El. 726.0 to El. 722.0) is within 12% of the closed-form
   requirement.

   9.3 Adopted Embedment and Adjustment
   The provided embedment of 4.0 ft below subgrade is adopted with the tieback capacity increased to compensate
   for the 12% shortfall in the passive contribution, per USACE EM 1110-2-2504 §5-3. The revised tieback force
   with the passive design contribution reduced to the mobilizable-at-4 ft value:
         T_adjusted = P_A − P_p,available_at_4ft
                        = 17,844 − 0.88 · 2,490
                        = 17,844 − 2,191
                        = 15,653 lb/ft
                        ≈ 15.7 kips/ft            (governs anchor design in Section 10)
   The 4.0 ft embedment is retained, tieback capacity governed by 15.7 kips/ft, and the resulting wall bending
   demand is carried into Section 11. Toe embedment OK with adjusted anchor demand.

   SECTION 11 — SHEET-PILE STRUCTURAL SECTION CHECK
   11.1 Governing Moment
   From Section 7.4, the net moment demand at the tieback-anchor arm is 188,223 ft-lb/ft. The maximum bending
   moment in the sheet-pile section (span between tieback and passive block) is developed at the point of zero shear
   on the elastic line, computed as follows.
   Locating the point of zero shear (measured downward from tieback at El. 745.5), where the accumulated
   horizontal load equals the tieback reaction (15,354 lb/ft):
   At depth y below tieback (y measured from El. 745.5):
       • Surcharge contribution: 83 · y
       • Earth pressure triangle contribution: (0.5) · (39.6 · (y + 4.5)²) − (0.5) · (39.6 · 4.5²)
   Solving numerically for the depth at which cumulative active pressure integrates to 15,354 lb/ft:
       • y = 14.6 ft below tieback (at approximately El. 730.9)
   At this location, the moment in the sheet-pile section (computed as the tieback reaction times its arm minus the
   load times its centroid arm above the section) is:
   M_max ≈ 71.2 kip-ft/ft of wall

   11.2 Sheet-Pile Section Selected
   Rev.1 adopts PZ-35 hot-rolled steel sheet piling (upgraded from PZ-27 in Rev.0), Grade 50 (Fy = 50 ksi):

               Property                                                   PZ-35
               Section modulus per foot of wall, S                        48.5 in³/ft
               Moment of inertia per foot of wall, I                      681 in4/ft
               Weight                                                     35.0 lb/ft² of wall face
               Web thickness                                              0.500 in
               Nominal flange width                                       22.6 in

   Allowable bending stress (AISC 360-16 Ch. F, Fy = 50 ksi, Ω = 1.67):
         F_b,allow = 0.6 · Fy = 30 ksi

         M_allow = F_b,allow · S = 30 · 48.5 = 1,455 kip-in/ft = 121 kip-ft/ft
   FS_bending = M_allow / M_max = 121 / 71.2 = 1.70 > 1.5 required — OK

   SECTION 13 — CONSTRUCTION SEQUENCE AND MONITORING
   13.1 Sequence
   1. Site preparation, layout of sheet-pile line, and pre-drilling for tip clearance where required.
   2. Contractor's dewatering system (Submittal 31-DW-A) started and confirmed operating at least 14 days prior to
   excavation, per that submittal's Article 3.3.
   3. Sheet piles driven from working grade El. 750.0 to tip El. 722.0 in continuous perimeter sequence.
   4. Excavate first lift to El. 745.0 (5 ft below working grade); expose tieback horizon at El. 745.5.
   5. Drill, install, grout, and proof-test tieback anchors on a 6-ft-o.c. horizontal pattern. Lock off each anchor at
   75% of design (75 kips per anchor).
   6. Continue excavation in maximum 4-ft vertical lifts to subgrade El. 726.0.
   7. Continuous monitoring of wall deflection (Section 13.2) during excavation.
   8. Construct plant-room mat and walls per structural documents (by others).
   9. Remove tieback anchors and detension per specification prior to backfill; sheet piling to be extracted or cut off
   at the elevation of the completed plant-room slab, per Contractor's option and coordination with the Owner's
   Representative.
